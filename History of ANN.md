This is the vast research history of ANNs, given in a few crucial points:

- In 1943, Neurophysiologist Warren McCulloch and mathematician Walter Pitts designed the first model of an artificial neuron, proving that simple neural networks can perform logical tasks.
- Psychologist Donald Hebb introduced _Hebbian Learning_ in 1949, the foundational concept that states neural pathways are strengthened through repeated use.
- Frank Rosenblatt developed the **Perceptron** at Cornell University in 1957, which was the first functional artificial neural network capable of learning to recognize patterns through simple binary classification.
- AI pioneers Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert published the book _Perceptrons_ in 1969, mathematically proving that single-layer neural networks could not process basic non-linear problems (such as the XOR logical function). For this the research of ANNs halted for nearly a decade.
- In the 1980s, John Hopfield introduced networks capable of associative memory. Concurrently, architectures like the Neocognitron laid the groundwork for visual pattern recognition.
- **1986 (Backpropagation):** Researchers (notably David Rumelhart, Geoffrey Hinton, and Ronald Williams) popularized **backpropagation**, an algorithm that allowed networks to adjust multiple hidden layers, finally solving the XOR problem.
- **The Slump:** Despite backpropagation, networks struggled with complex, multi-layered data. The "vanishing gradient" problem made deep networks difficult to train, and simpler statistical models like Support Vector Machines (SVMs) became the industry standard.
- **2006 (Greedy Layer-Wise Pre-training):** Geoffrey Hinton and Ruslan Salakhutdinov introduced "Deep Belief Networks," proving that deep multi-layer neural networks could be effectively trained.
- **2012 (AlexNet Breakthrough):** A convolutional neural network (CNN) designed by Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Geoffrey Hinton shattered image-recognition records at the ImageNet competition. This milestone popularized modern **Deep Learning**.
- **Transformers & LLMs (2017):** Researchers at Google introduced the **Transformer architecture** (relying on 'attention' mechanisms), allowing networks to process the context of words in parallel.
- **Generative AI Dominance:** This architecture scaled up to create the highly advanced Large Language Models (LLMs) and diffusion models that power AI applications today.
